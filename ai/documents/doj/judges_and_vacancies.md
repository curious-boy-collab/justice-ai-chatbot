# Judges Strength, Appointment Process, and Vacancy Data

> **Retrieval Date:** 2026-11-08  
> **Verification Status:** Verified Official Government Data (Figures represent baseline official statistics; subject to dynamic updates)

---

## 1. Supreme Court of India: Sanctioned Strength and Appointment
- **Sanctioned Strength:** 34 Judges (including the Chief Justice of India) pursuant to the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2019.
- **Constitutional Basis:** Appointments are made by the President of India under Article 124(2) of the Constitution based on the recommendation of the Supreme Court Collegium.
- **Retirement Age:** 65 years.

---

## 2. High Courts: Structure and Vacancies
- India currently has **25 High Courts** serving States and Union Territories.
- **Total Approved Strength:** 1,114 Judges across all 25 High Courts (Permanent + Additional Judges).
- **Appointment Procedure:**
  - Governed by the Memorandum of Procedure (MoP) framed pursuant to the Three Judges Cases.
  - High Court Collegium recommends names to the Chief Minister / Governor, Union Minister of Law & Justice, and the Supreme Court Collegium.
  - Warrants of appointment are issued by the President of India under Article 217 / 224.
- **Retirement Age:** 62 years.
- **Vacancy Dynamics:** Vacancies fluctuate continuously due to retirements, resignations, elevations, and new appointment notifications published in the Gazette of India by the Department of Justice.

---

## 3. District and Subordinate Judiciary
- **Administrative Jurisdiction:** The recruitment, promotion, and service conditions of District and Subordinate Judges fall under the administrative jurisdiction of the respective High Courts and State Governments under Articles 233, 234, and 235 of the Constitution.
- **Sanctioned vs Working Strength (Approximate National Benchmark):**
  - Approved Strength: ~25,000 judicial officers nationwide.
  - Working Strength: ~19,000 to ~20,000 judicial officers.
  - Ongoing recruitments are conducted through State Judicial Services Examinations.

---

## 4. How Citizens and Litigants Can Verify Live Vacancies
- The Department of Justice publishes monthly vacancy reports on [https://doj.gov.in](https://doj.gov.in).
- Real-time judge strength figures across subordinate courts can be verified on the National Judicial Data Grid (NJDG) at [https://njdg.ecourts.gov.in](https://njdg.ecourts.gov.in).
- Citizens must be informed that vacancy numbers change with every presidential appointment notification.
