## 1. Constitutional Provisions for Judicial Appointments
The appointment of judges to the higher judiciary in India is governed by the Constitution of India:
- **Article 124(2):** Appointment of the Chief Justice of India and Judges of the Supreme Court of India by the President of India.
- **Article 217(1):** Appointment of Chief Justices and Judges of High Courts by the President of India.
- **Article 224:** Appointment of Additional and Acting Judges of High Courts.
- **Article 222:** Transfer of a High Court Judge from one High Court to another.
- **Articles 233, 234 & 235:** Recruitment and appointment of District and Subordinate Judges under the administrative superintendence of the respective High Courts and State Public Service Commissions.

## 2. The Collegium System: Structure and Working Mechanism
The Collegium System evolved through the landmark judgments in the *Three Judges Cases* (*S.P. Gupta (1981)*, *Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association (1993)*, and *Special Reference No. 1 of 1998*):

### A. Supreme Court Collegium
- **Composition for SC Appointments:** Chief Justice of India (CJI) and the **four senior-most** puisne Judges of the Supreme Court (5-member body).
- **Composition for HC Appointments & Transfers:** Chief Justice of India and the **two senior-most** puisne Judges of the Supreme Court (3-member body).
- **Seniority & Elevation:** The senior-most judge of the Supreme Court is traditionally appointed as the Chief Justice of India unless considered unfit.

### B. High Court Collegium
- Comprises the Chief Justice of the respective High Court and the **two senior-most** puisne Judges of that High Court.
- Initiates proposals for appointment of judges from the Bar and State Judicial Services.

## 3. The Memorandum of Procedure (MoP) Workflow
1. **Initiation:** The High Court Chief Justice initiates recommendations at least 6 months prior to an anticipated vacancy.
2. **Consultation:** The recommendation is sent simultaneously to the Chief Minister and the Governor of the State.
3. **Union Government Review:** The Governor/CM transmits comments to the Union Minister of Law & Justice, Department of Justice. The Union Government conducts background verification through the Intelligence Bureau (IB).
4. **Supreme Court Scrutiny:** The dossier is submitted to the Chief Justice of India and the Supreme Court Collegium.
5. **Presidential Warrant:** Upon acceptance, the recommendation is forwarded to the Prime Minister, who advises the President of India to issue the formal Warrant of Appointment published in the Gazette of India.

## 4. Anti-Hallucination Mandate on Vacancy Statistics
- **Crucial Rule:** The JusticeAI Assistant must never invent, guess, or extrapolate judge vacancies.
- While the approved benchmark strength is:
  - **Supreme Court:** 34 Judges.
  - **High Courts:** 1,114 Judges across 25 High Courts.
  - **District Judiciary:** ~25,000 sanctioned judicial posts.
- Live vacancies change regularly due to retirements and fresh appointments. Litigants and citizens must always be guided to the monthly official vacancy reports published on [https://doj.gov.in](https://doj.gov.in).
